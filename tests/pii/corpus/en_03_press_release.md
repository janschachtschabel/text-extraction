# Press release: new charging points for the county

Bristol, 26 October 2026. The county council and the municipal utility today presented the expansion of public charging infrastructure. By the end of next year, 120 new charging points will be built across all towns and villages. The project is part of the regional energy transition strategy and is funded by the Department for Transport and the European Union.

Council leader Katherine Rutter said at the presentation in County Hall: "We want every family in our county to be able to charge without detours. The shift to electric transport only works if it works in everyday life." The managing director of the utility, Bernard Kowalski, added that the first twenty sites will go live in the spring. Locations were chosen deliberately so that they are easy to reach without a car, such as railway stations, schools and town halls.

Mr Kowalski also stressed that the electricity is to come from regional wind farms and solar plants. The partnership with the University of Bath supplies the measurement data. Researcher Dr. Imogen Vale explained that the utilisation of each charger will be published in real time so that residents can plan their journeys better.

The Taxpayers' Alliance reacted cautiously. Its spokesman Matthew Engle pointed out that running costs must be covered in the long term. The opposition on the council, represented by Mrs Schroeder, called for more transparency in the awarding of contracts.
